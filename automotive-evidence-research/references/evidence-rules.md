# Evidence Rules

## Evidence states

- **Confirmed:** A primary or authoritative source directly supports the exact claim, or multiple strong independent sources converge.
- **High-confidence inference:** Evidence supports the conclusion indirectly; expose the reasoning chain and alternatives.
- **Unknown:** Public evidence is missing, conflicting, version-ambiguous, or too weak.

Never promote an inference to confirmed because it is plausible.

## Source grades

1. **A:** Regulation, standard, patent text, peer-reviewed paper, certification record, official technical manual, test report with method.
2. **B:** Company product page, filing, press kit, official presentation, named executive or engineer interview.
3. **C:** Reputable engineering publication, teardown organization, industry analyst, specialist media with attributable evidence.
4. **D:** Forum, social post, aggregator, unattributed repost. Use to discover leads, not as sole support for material claims.

## Corroboration

- For a material quantitative or strategic claim, seek two non-common-origin sources and at least one A/B source.
- Treat copied press releases and articles citing the same unnamed source as one source chain.
- When only one source exists, label it explicitly.
- Match each citation to the exact sentence it supports.

## Conflicts

Preserve both values, then test version, date, geography, hardware generation, peak versus continuous rating, theoretical versus measured value, and test condition. Do not average unresolved conflicts.

## Citation quality

Prefer the deepest stable source URL. Record publisher, title, date, page/figure where applicable, and access date. Avoid citing search-result pages.
